I. History under Rome

I. Under Roman rule, Ephesus became an administrative center for the Roman Province of Asia. It operated as an administrative center being a part of the Roman imperial system while preserving the Greek language and ways of life, bringing both cultures together. Because of its massive trading economy, Ephesus became very wealthy under Roman rule, enabling trade throughout Europe, Asia, and into Africa.

II. Geography and resources

II. Located in a valley near the Aegean coast, Ephesus had a natural border from the hills and mountains while being an epicenter for trade throughout Asia Minor. The climate during the different seasons allowed for the cultivation of different crops such as olives, grapes, cereals, etc. Timber surrounded the city, making it a valuable resource for building homes within the city's walls and also allowing for its trade using the maritime trade routes on the Aegean Sea. The fertile ground, coastline access, and important natural resource placement are all reasons that Ephesus was able to prosper under the Roman Empire and make the city an epicenter for trade, business, and growth.

V. Famous building

V. The Library of Celsus was built in 114-117 CE after Roman Senator Tiberius Julius Celsus Polemaeanus. Aside from being his burial place, it also served as a collection of roughly 12000 scrolls. Due to this large amount of writing being saved, the library was vital in the rise of Christianity during this time. In 262 CE, a fire during the Gothic invasion destroyed the library, leaving it in ruins to be rebuilt.
